# Content review — September 5, 2026

Brackish bundles original concise summaries, not copied guidebooks. Links in every
spot/species detail connect the summary to its source. Search works on this bundle;
it is not an API of current catches. All seven map pins are approximate location
references for exploration; they are not surveyed access points or instructions
to cross private land. Posted signs and current source information take priority.

## Species field marks

Marine notes use NOAA Fisheries' species pages for
[striped bass](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/atlantic-striped-bass),
[bluefish](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/bluefish),
[summer flounder](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/summer-flounder),
[scup](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/scup), and
[black sea bass](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/black-sea-bass).

Freshwater identification references include
[DEC's largemouth bass spotlight](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NYSDEC/bulletins/3a1112e),
[Minnesota DNR sunfish identification](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/fish/sunfish/biology.html),
[New York Sea Grant's fish guide](https://seagrant.sunysb.edu/nysportfishing/fishid.htm),
[DEC crappie guidance](https://dec.ny.gov/things-to-do/freshwater-fishing/learn-to-fish/tips-skills/fishing-for-crappie),
and [DEC carp guidance](https://dec.ny.gov/things-to-do/freshwater-fishing/places-to-fish/statewide-opportunities/carp).
The brown bullhead occurrence reference is Van Cortlandt Lake; basic field marks
are identification guidance rather than a claim of diagnostic certainty.
Largemouth nomenclature follows the cited DEC material (Micropterus salmoides);
newer taxonomic treatments may use different names. Common-name matching is the
user-facing convention, not a taxonomic authority.

The first link audit caught three guessed DEC slugs returning 404. They were
replaced with the verified references above. See source-link-audit-final.json
for final HTTP results, which establish reachability at review time only.

## Regulations and health

- [DEC Region 2 special freshwater rules](https://dec.ny.gov/things-to-do/freshwater-fishing/regulations/region-2-special-fishing)
  establishes catch-and-release requirements for the selected city lakes.
- DEC lake pages describe barbless hooks and non-lead weights under NYC park rules.
- [Current saltwater regulations](https://dec.ny.gov/things-to-do/saltwater-fishing/recreational-fishing-regulations)
  is linked rather than freezing a table of seasons, possession limits and sizes.
  The reviewed page states regulations changed May 12, 2026 and may change again.
- [NYC fish consumption advisories](https://www.health.ny.gov/environmental/outdoors/fish/health_advisories/regional/new_york_city.htm)
  is linked for current health advice. No photograph, mascot copy or model output
  says a fish is safe to eat. Occasional automated-fetch failures are not treated
  as evidence the advisory does not exist.
- [DEC general freshwater rules](https://dec.ny.gov/things-to-do/freshwater-fishing/regulations/general)
  describes immediate release of protected/out-of-season fish and fish handling.

Tackle is purpose-based editorial guidance, not a recommendation to buy a named
product. The only shopping route is an Apple Maps search for NYC tackle shops.
There are no inventory, price, availability, affiliate or retailer-partnership claims.
No weather, tide, stocking feed, live catch report or objective 'best spot' ranking
is supplied. Near me orders by straight-line distance only.
